01 — Mechanism

Two cups.
One sphere.

The cups glide together on a centre axis while the band lowers into the crown. Bearings are sealed and serviceable, so the gesture stays consistent over time.

Hemispherical cups

Each cup is half of the closed sphere. The shell, cushion and driver are arranged so the two halves meet without a visible gap.

Twin rails

The headband descends on two parallel rails. It does not rotate or detach, so the closed shape is predictable every time.

One seam

The centre seam is the only line left when Echo is closed. A small LED sits below it, subtle enough to read as a detail rather than a display.

02 — Bio-Energy Technology

Begin with
the body.

A framework for the relationship between body, material, sound and space. It informs proportions and contact pressure. It is not a battery or a medical claim.

01 / Fit

Pressure, mapped first

Weight is balanced across the crown and temples before the shell is shaped. Comfort is a design input, not an afterthought.

02 / Material

Named for what it is

Anodised aluminium, memory foam and recycled textile. Materials are chosen for how they age against the body.

03 / Sound

Room before speaker

Tuning starts with silence: the space between notes, the edge of a room, the quiet after a track ends.

Bio-Energy is a design framework, not a medical or energy-harvesting technology.

03 — Materials & care

Built to be kept.
Not replaced.

Echo is designed around service. The mechanism can be opened, the cushions can be replaced and the battery can be cared for. Wear is expected; disposal is not.
